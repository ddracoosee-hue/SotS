# 21 — TARGET AUDIENCE RESEARCH AND THE READER MIND MODEL

**Audience: adults only.** The book is written for high-level, reflective **young adults
and adults**. No persona, metric, or design choice in SotS targets teenagers or children.

> **Status of this document:** research compiled on 2026-09-28 to ground the design. Every
> factual line below is itself a claim that SotS must **fact-check in its first live run**
> (task T23.010). Treat the citations as leads that are expected to be accurate, not as

## 1. Who the readers are

| Cohort | Birth years | Age in 2026 | Role in SotS |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gen Z adults** | 1997–2008 | 18–29 | **Primary** audience (≈ 60% of panel weight) |
| **Millennials** | 1981–1996 | 30–45 | **Secondary** audience (≈ 30%) |
| **Gen X** | 1965–1980 | 46–61 | **Outside adult perspective** (≈ 10%): checks the book doesn't read as generationally narrow |

Cohort boundaries follow Pew Research Center (Millennials 1981–1996; Gen Z 1997–2012). Gen Z
is **cut at 2008** so every persona is 18+.
- Pew: https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2019/01/17/where-millennials-end-and-generation-z-begins/

**Reader profile the author has specified:** high-level thinking adults. For SotS this means:
readers who scrutinize arguments, notice weak evidence, resent being talked down to, and
value nuance over slogans. In research terms, a **high "need for cognition"** audience (§2.5).

## 2. Research findings that shape the design

### 2.1 Emerging adulthood: the life stage of the primary cohort
Arnett (2000) describes ages ~18–25 (often extended to 29) as *emerging adulthood*, with five
features: **identity exploration, instability, self-focus, feeling in-between, and a sense of
possibilities.**
→ *Design implication:* chapters should speak to identity questions, change, and possibility
without assuming settled careers, relationships, or finances. Persona cards encode life-stage
instability.
- https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/psychology/emerging-adulthood

### 2.2 The emotional baseline: many readers are struggling
- NSDUH (SAMHSA/NIMH): adults 18–25 had the **highest prevalence of any mental illness, 36.2%**
  (vs 29.4% for ages 26–49), per the most recent year NIMH reports (2022 data).
  https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/statistics/mental-illness
- Harvard Making Caring Common (2021): **61% of young adults (18–25)** reported "serious
  loneliness", vs 36% of all Americans.
  https://mcc.gse.harvard.edu/reports/loneliness-in-america

→ *Design implication:* assume a meaningful share of readers are anxious, lonely, or
depressed. The text must be **non-pathologizing, non-shaming, and safe**, and must never
diagnose the reader. Persona panels include readers in difficult seasons (§20).

### 2.3 Readers live in a misinformation-heavy self-help ecosystem
- A 2022 *Canadian Journal of Psychiatry* study of the top 100 TikTok ADHD videos: **52%
  misleading**, 27% personal experience only, 21% useful.
  https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/07067437221082854
- PlushCare (2022, a company analysis, not peer-reviewed; weight it lower): **83.7%** of 500
  #mentalhealthtips/#mentalhealthadvice TikTok videos were rated misleading.
  https://plushcare.com/blog/tiktok-mental-health

→ *Design implication:* this audience has seen oversimplified "therapy-speak". SotS's
verified, labeled evidence (06) is a **credibility differentiator**. The tone must avoid
clinical-sounding claims without support. The Audience Lab measures *credibility* and a
*"sounds like TikTok therapy"* flag (20 §3).

### 2.4 Reading culture
- ALA / Portland State (2022 survey, n ≈ 2,075): **54%** of Gen Z and Millennials visited a
  physical library in the past year; they read and bought print books more than any other
  format. BookTok is a major discovery channel. **43% don't identify as "readers"**.
  Primary: https://www.ala.org/sites/default/files/advocacy/content/tools/Gen-Z-and-Millennials-Report%20(1).pdf
  (An ALA news page with "2019" in its URL also describes this report. **Verify the survey year
  against the PDF** before citing it in the book.)

→ *Design implication:* write for print-loving readers **and** for "non-readers" who pick up
a book because of a clip. **Quotable, shareable lines** matter (the pacing analyst tracks
"pull-quote density"), but never at the expense of depth.

### 2.5 High-level thinkers are persuaded by argument quality
- The Elaboration Likelihood Model (Petty & Cacioppo, 1986): persuasion runs through a
  *central route* (careful scrutiny of the arguments) or a *peripheral route* (cues).
  People high in **need for cognition** (Cacioppo & Petty, 1982) discriminate more between
  strong and weak arguments.
  https://richardepetty.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/1986-advances-pettycacioppo.pdf

→ *Design implication:* for this audience, **weak evidence actively backfires**. SotS
pairs every lesson with a story *and* a sound argument/evidence, and the persona panel
includes an "intellectual respect" metric.

### 2.6 Stories lower resistance: narrative transportation
- Green & Brock (2000): readers highly "transported" into a narrative counter-argue less. van
  Laer et al. (2014) meta-analyzed 132 effect sizes of narrative transportation.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_theory_(psychology)
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0148296318305356

→ *Design implication:* **story before lesson**. Specific sensory and emotional detail,
characters the reader can identify with (including the author's past self), and a narrative
arc per chapter.

### 2.7 Commands trigger pushback: psychological reactance
- Controlling language ("must", "should", "ought") raises reactance. Autonomy-supportive
  language that offers choice ("you might", "one option") reduces it (Miller et al., 2007,
  *Human Communication Research*; meta-analyses in HCR, 2025).
  https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ755929
  https://academic.oup.com/hcr/advance-article/doi/10.1093/hcr/hqaf016/8178818

→ *Design implication:* lessons are **offered, not ordered**. The tonality analyst computes a
*controlling-language index* (20 §3). Personas rate "preachiness".

### 2.8 Affirm before you challenge: self-affirmation
- Self-affirmation (reflecting on values/strengths) reduces defensive processing of
  threatening information (Epton et al., 2015 meta-analysis, *Health Psychology*).
  https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/The-impact-of-self-affirmation-on-health-behavior-a-Epton-Harris/1fceab3cfab51ebdd21241ce170c05582b7e612f

→ *Design implication:* before a hard truth, the text affirms the reader's values or
competence. This is a named playbook technique (20 §7).

### 2.9 Attention is fragmented, but that is not a reason to simplify
- Gloria Mark (UC Irvine): average attention on a single *screen* before switching fell from
  ~2.5 minutes (2004) to ~47 seconds (studies 2014–2020). **This measures task-switching on
  screens, not a biological attention limit.**
  https://gloriamark.com/attention-span/

→ *Design implication:* clear structure, signposting, and payoffs at a steady rhythm
(section-level "hooks"), while **keeping intellectual depth**. The pacing analyst measures
structure, not "dumbing down".

### 2.10 Synthetic personas are useful but biased
- Argyle et al. (2023) showed that LLMs conditioned on demographic backstories can approximate
  some survey distributions ("silicon samples"). Later work found this weakens without shortcut
  variables, that simulated groups show **lower variance** than real people, and that personas
  can collapse into **stereotyped caricatures**.
  https://cjbarrie.github.io/GenAI_Soc/week07_silicon_sampling.html
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.02919

→ *Design implication:* the persona panel is a **rehearsal tool, not a substitute for real
readers.** SotS enforces anti-caricature rules, variance monitoring, and calibration against
real beta readers (20 §6–7).

## 3. The Reader Mind Model: what the book tries to invoke

Every chapter is designed to move the reader through a **Reader Journey** of seven mental
states. These are the "mental processes and patterns" SotS aims to invoke, measure, and
reproduce.

| # | State | What the reader thinks/feels | Mechanism (§2) | Text techniques | How SotS measures it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| J1 | **Recognition** | "That's me / I've seen this." | identification, emerging adulthood | concrete, specific opening scene; naming a common experience precisely | persona `recognition` (0–10); "felt seen" quote extraction |
| J2 | **Safety** | "I'm not being judged." | self-affirmation, low reactance | affirm values/effort before challenge; no shaming; the author's own vulnerability first | persona `felt_judged` (inverted); tonality warmth; controlling-language index |
| J3 | **Curiosity / tension** | "Wait, why is that?" | central-route engagement | an open question, contradiction, or surprising fact (verified) | persona `curiosity`; pacing hook detection |
| J4 | **Transportation** | absorbed in the story | narrative transportation | scene, sensory detail, emotional honesty, arc | persona `absorption`; emotion engine intensity curve |
| J5 | **Insight / reframe** | "I never thought about it that way." | argument quality (ELM), evidence | reframe backed by labeled evidence; acknowledging complexity | persona `insight` + `intellectual_respect`; message reception |
| J6 | **Agency** | "I could try this, and it's my choice." | autonomy support, self-efficacy | options, not orders; small concrete steps | persona `agency`, `preachiness` (inverted); controlling-language index |
| J7 | **Integration** | "This stays with me." | reflection | reflection prompt, memorable line, callback to the opening | persona `takeaway` match to the core message; `would_share` |

### 3.1 The Reader Journey Spec (per chapter)
Each chapter brief (after OI-01) gets a `reader_journey` block, set together with the author:
```yaml
reader_journey:
  ch03:
    target_message: "C3.1"
    j1_recognition:   {anchor_hint: "opening scene at the bus stop", target: 7.5}
    j2_safety:        {target: 8.0}
    j3_curiosity:     {target: 7.0}
    j4_transportation:{target: 7.5}
    j5_insight:       {target: 8.0}
    j6_agency:        {target: 7.5, max_preachiness: 3.0}
    j7_integration:   {target_reception_rate: 0.80}
```
The Audience Lab (20) scores each chapter against its spec. The Journey Map view shows each
state's score in chapter order, making it obvious where readers fall off.

### 3.2 Ethics of invoking mental states
- The aim is **understanding and agency**, never manipulation: no false urgency, no
  fear-mongering, no invented statistics, no dark patterns.
- Safety (J2) outranks every other target. If an edit raises absorption but lowers safety,
  it is rejected.
- Readers in crisis are not the target of persuasion techniques. The book includes resources
  (safety.yaml) where appropriate, and the legal/health counsel (22) reviews all
  mental-health advice.
